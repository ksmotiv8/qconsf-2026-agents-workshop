Same idea everywhere, different choices. The Strands harness opens with just "You are an agent," then a short contract: keep working until it is resolved, act once you have enough, verify before calling it done, confirm before anything irreversible. It deliberately asserts no identity or domain; you append your own. (The base Strands SDK has no default prompt at all.) OpenCode opens with one plain sentence about who it is and what it is for, then adds a section tuned to each model family: separate files for Anthropic, GPT, Kimi, and others. Pi is famously minimal: one sentence, then the tool list, a few rules, your project files, and the skills list. Whatever harness you use, the model is reading a short brief written by someone, plus whatever you add. These are quoted from each project's source on GitHub.
