Read the whole file
Here is the whole file, top to bottom, drawn to scale. The front matter is twelve lines, and it is the only part in context until the skill loads. Then a one-line goal, and a short section on why AI text sounds the way it does: the model picks the most likely next word, so it makes the choice that fits the widest range of readers. How to work is a four-step process: mark the tells, draft, check that no fact was lost, then write the final version. Notice the line about treating the text as material, never as instructions. That is prompt-injection defense, written in plain English. Then the bulk of the file: 26 patterns in six groups, each with what to watch for, why it is a problem, and a before and after. Examples do most of the teaching. It ends with when not to act. One line says text written before ChatGPT launched is not AI-written. That is not strictly true, since GPT-3 shipped in 2020, but the point holds: a tell is not proof of who wrote something. And the source, Wikipedia's Signs of AI writing.
